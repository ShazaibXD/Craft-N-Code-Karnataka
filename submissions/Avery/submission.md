## Project summary

MINDTRACE finds out why a student got a problem wrong (a careless slip, a unit error, or a missing prerequisite), asks one short question only when the evidence is thin, teaches just the missing concept, and checks it with a fresh problem. It is for students preparing for exams such as JEE, NEET, GATE and first-year engineering.

**1. What made you choose this problem?**

We kept noticing that a wrong answer in a numerical-heavy subject can mean two very different things: a slip on the last line, or a missing idea from several chapters back. Every app we’d used responds the same way to both. Docking points teaches nothing about a slip, and rewatching a lecture wastes time on a gap that sits in one small prerequisite.

We chose problems where this is most visible: stacked prerequisites and multi-step numericals. That’s why we cover JEE, NEET, GATE and first-year B.Tech. The method (error signature → prerequisite graph → probe) doesn’t depend on the subject, though our deepest worked example is first-year transformers.

**2. Who is it for, and how would they use it in a normal week?**

It’s for students who practise by solving numerical problems: JEE, NEET and GATE aspirants, and first-year engineering students. Here is how we imagine a week:

- Weekdays (about 20–30 minutes): work the day’s problems in the Problem Workspace and type the working steps. A slip gets a quick retry with no penalty. A suspected gap gets a one-step probe, then a roughly 60-second micro-lesson if it’s confirmed.
- Anytime: paste a question the syllabus tracks don’t cover into Ask Anything and get its prerequisite map.
- Weekend: open the Dashboard. Red nodes show what to fix first, and the spaced-review queue brings back concepts that have gone stale.

**3. If you had another month, what would you build next?**

The biggest limit today is that Ask Anything works from the model’s general knowledge, not the student’s own material.

- Upload your own resources. Students add their notes, textbook chapters or a past paper (PDF). We’d build the prerequisite map from their syllabus and write probes and micro-lessons grounded in their material, with a pointer to the page that explains the gap.
- Calibrate BKT from real data. The slip, guess and learning values (0.10, 0.20, 0.25) are fixed priors today. With real attempts we’d fit them per concept.
- Photo-of-working input. Students snap their handwritten solution and we extract the steps, so they don’t have to retype them.
- Teacher cohort view. A class-level heatmap of the most common root-cause gaps.
